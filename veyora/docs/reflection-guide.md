# Personal Reflection — guide for Krisha (400–500 words)

This section of the final report must be written by **Krisha Mange in her own
words**, from her actual experience. Nothing here is pre-written for her, and
no attempt is made to disguise assisted drafting — authenticity is the point.

## Suggested structure (~4 blocks of 100–130 words)

### 1. Starting point and planning
* What felt hardest when you read the brief — the deadline conflict, the
  scope, or the risk requirement? Why?
* How did you decide what to build and, more importantly, what to **cut**?
* What did writing the risk register *before* coding change about how you
  worked? (e.g. the duplicate-booking risk became a database constraint
  early.)

### 2. What building it taught you
* Pick one concrete technical moment: e.g. watching two concurrent bookings
  race and one get HTTP 409, or fixing a CSRF/login interaction. What did you
  try, what failed, what finally worked?
* How did testing against an isolated database per test change your
  confidence in the results?

### 3. What you would do differently
* One scope decision you'd revisit (e.g. reminders, the task board, the
  plan view).
* One process decision (e.g. earlier JIRA setup, earlier manual browser
  checks, recording actual hours from day one).

### 4. SDG 3 and honest evidence
* Why does "a little less complexity" matter for good health and well-being?
* What did keeping evidence honest (no invented test counts, no fake JIRA
  screenshots, separating estimated from actual effort) cost you — and what
  did it buy you?
* One sentence on what you'd tell next year's class about risk-aware
  planning.

## Ground rules

* Write from what actually happened. If something didn't go to plan (deadlines,
  bugs, lost time), say so — the Analysis section of the report already values
  honesty over polish.
* 400–500 words. First person. No need for citations.
* Paste the finished text into the final report's *Project Reflection*
  chapter, replacing the placeholder.
